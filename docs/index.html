Software protection strategy / 01

Protect the value.
Not just the binary.

A layered plan to reduce license abuse and redistribution—without making legitimate customers pay the price in friction, privacy or reliability.

PROPOSAL · OCTOBER 22, 2026 · SIX-WEEK ESTIMATE
Defense in depth

Seven layers. No silver bullet.

Prioritize authorization and remote value first. Treat client hardening as a supporting control, never the foundation.

01 / FOUNDATION

Licensing

Signed licenses, activation, entitlement APIs and revocation to reduce keygen-style abuse and unmanaged redistribution.

Never ship signing secrets. Online check-in bounds the revocation delay.

02 / SEATS

Device identity

Pseudonymous registration and optional binding to reduce sharing, cloned environments and seat abuse.

Assume spoofing is possible. Offer resets; minimize hardware collection.

03 / CORE VALUE

Server-side control

Keep premium APIs, model weights, billing and critical authorization behind controlled services.

Plan for connectivity, latency and service outages from day one.

04 / FRICTION

Tamper resistance

Selective obfuscation, integrity checks, signed updates and a proportionate anti-debugging review.

Raises attacker cost; does not promise an uncrackable client.

05 / CONDITIONAL

Streaming & content

Where media is in scope: supported platform DRM, forensic watermarking and concurrent-stream limits.

Watermarks support tracing. They cannot prevent every capture.

06 / SIGNALS

Detection

Minimal telemetry, activation anomaly rules and consumption analysis to identify suspicious usage.

Start in observation mode. Corroborate signals before sanctions.

07 / RESPONSE

Enforcement

Preserve evidence, monitor material redistribution and route provider notices through authorized review.

Prioritize meaningful harm, reversible actions and customer appeals.

Trust boundaries

The server grants. The client verifies.

Billing → Entitlements → Signed lease → Device verification → Authorized service

Authenticate billing events, reserve seats atomically and issue short-lived leases through a separate signing service. Premium APIs independently check identity, entitlement, quotas and revocation.

Proposed policy: renew every 24 hours, with a documented 72-hour offline allowance for local features. Remote services require current authorization. Offline revocation waits for expiry or reconnection.

Delivery sequence

From threat model to measured rollout.

Owner roles and timings are proposals; confirm staffing and product requirements before committing.

WEEK 01

Define the boundaries

Product + security · Assets, abuse model, privacy review and offline policy.

Gate: approved threat model, data inventory and connectivity rules.

WEEKS 02–03

Build authorization

Backend + billing · Entitlements, signing, activation, renewal and revocation.

Gate: invalid leases rejected, race-safe seat caps and idempotent billing events.

WEEK 04

Integrate the client

Client + platform · Verification, renewal, device management and recovery.

Gate: offline expiry, clock changes, device resets and outages tested.

WEEK 05

Observe and prepare

Security + operations · Minimal telemetry, anomaly review and key runbooks.

Gate: retention checked, false positives measured and key rotation rehearsed.

WEEK 06

Harden and pilot

Release + support · Selective hardening, staged rollout and support playbooks.

Gate: signed updates, rollback drill and pilot approval.

Customer-first constraints

Protection that earns trust.

Privacy & recovery

  • Use pseudonymous device references; avoid raw hardware inventories by default.
  • Propose 30-day raw-event and 90-day aggregate retention, subject to review.
  • Provide device removal, recovery, clear errors and an appeal route.
  • Require corroboration and human review for consequential enforcement.

Measure before tightening

  • Baseline activation success, API latency and renewal failures.
  • Track false positives, seat resets and support burden—not just blocks.
  • Test outages, webhook replay, seat races, revocation and key compromise.
  • Stage enforcement with a rollback that never bypasses server authentication.
Decisions before implementation

Resolve the policy, then ship the controls.

Confirm offline features, seat limits, transfer allowances, supported platforms, media scope, operating budget and ownership of privacy, keys, incidents and appeals.
